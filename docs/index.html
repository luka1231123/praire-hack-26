Behind the Accounts

Finding state-run propaganda networks by how accounts act, not what they say, so AI rewording can't hide them.

The problem

Propaganda networks are real, big, and organized

Fake accounts that act together can sway what millions of people think is popular.

25,000
coordinated TikTok accounts behind a pro-Russian campaign

Romania annulled its 2024 presidential first round. The candidate went from under 1% in polls to first place.

7,700+
Facebook accounts removed from China's Spamouflage network

Meta's largest takedown ever. The accounts posted on an office-hours schedule in China's time zone.

3.6M
articles from Russia's Pravda network in 2024

Built to seed AI chatbots, which repeated its claims about a third of the time in an audit.

Why it's hard now

AI rewording beats text detectors

  • Copy-paste used to give networks away. AI rewrites every post, so there's no duplicate text left.
  • Reworded propaganda works better. In a 1,200-person study, AI-paraphrased messages made false claims seem widely believed.
  • AI-text detectors break. Over 90% accurate on raw AI text, they fail once it's paraphrased.
Don't ask "was this written by AI?"
Ask "are these accounts working together?"
Our approach

Five traces that rewording can't hide

No text model. Operators can change the words, but they still share behavior.

Same reply targets

Replying to the same messages as each other.

Same timing

Hitting the same post, link or tag within 60 seconds.

Same links & hashtags

Sharing the same URLs, #tags, @mentions or reposts.

Same template text

Identical posts once case, links, digits, punctuation and emoji are removed.

Same work schedule

Matching weekly activity, hour by hour, like Spamouflage's office hours.

Rare traces count more: a link 2 accounts share outweighs one shared by 50. One signal can be a coincidence; several point to a network.

Link strength = (10·text + 3·items + min(replies, 3) + min(fast, 1)) × schedule match. The weights come from how predictive each signal was on the labeled Telegram data.

Beyond the dataset

Runs live, judges behavior

Live on Bluesky

monitor.py reads Bluesky's public firehose (about 40 posts a second, free, no key), keeps a rolling 24-hour window, and refreshes the report every 5 minutes.

Behavior, not opinions

Like Meta's own policy, the tool flags fake accounts working together. It never scores what a post argues, so it doesn't police viewpoints.
