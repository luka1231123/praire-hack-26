How PropNET works

The short version: some people run many fake accounts at once. Those accounts give themselves away by copying each other: they post the same words, share the same links and are online at the same hours. PropNET finds accounts that copy each other and shows the proof, so a person can check it.

In more detail: it finds groups of accounts that act together, such as troll farms, state operators and spam networks. It uses no text model, because AI can reword every post. It looks at what operators can't easily change: who they reply to, what they link, and when they work.

How it works

Every pair of accounts is scored on five traces:

Same reply targets
They reply to the same messages.
Same timing
They hit the same post, link or tag within 60 seconds.
Same links and tags
They share URLs, hashtags, mentions or reposts. A trace counts 1/(sharers − 1), so a link two accounts share outweighs one shared by fifty.
Same template text
Their posts match once case, links, digits, punctuation and emoji are removed.
Same schedule
Their weekly activity, hour by hour, lines up. This multiplies the other traces.
  1. Every post becomes one row: account, time, text, reply target, links and tags.
  2. Each pair of accounts is scored on the traces above.
  3. Leiden clustering finds tight groups of three or more accounts.
  4. Groups are ranked by shared content per member, with the evidence attached.

Reading a group

The headline
How many accounts are in the group, and the thing most of them share, like the same words or the same link.
Clues ✓
How the accounts copy each other: same words, same links and hashtags, replies to the same posts, posting within a minute of each other, or being online at the same hours. On Bluesky there are also clues from the profiles: brand-new accounts, the same name, copy-paste or empty profiles, deleted accounts, posting nonstop, pushing one website, or never sleeping. Green signs like "Looks like real people" point the other way.
The orange bar
"Copies more than 94 of every 100 groups we found" compares the group with all the others in that dataset. It is a ranking, not the chance that the group is fake: a group lower on the list can still be a real network.
Answer key
Only where a dataset has one. On X, how many of the accounts had the same troll job. On Telegram, how many were known propaganda accounts. These are checked facts, not guesses.
Topic
What their posts are mostly about (politics, scams, adult content or other), from a small language model. It describes the topic, not who runs the group.
For investigators
Each group page has a closed section with the raw score, every shared trace, a timeline, the weekly schedule, the closest account pairs, the member list and downloads (CSV, GraphML, JSON).

Limits

It flags behavior, not opinions. It never scores what a post argues. A group can be a real community, a news feed or a fan account network. Open each post at its source and decide yourself.

The time zone shown on a group page comes from its quietest hours. It's a hint, not proof. The live Bluesky view covers only the last 24 hours, so small groups may be chance.

Datasets

Telegram
17.3M comments from 13 Russian channels, with 15,856 accounts labeled as propaganda by researchers (Kireev et al., USENIX Security 2025). Used to measure accuracy.
X: IRA trolls
2.9M tweets from 2,843 accounts of Russia's Internet Research Agency, each tagged with a role (FiveThirtyEight). Used to check that found groups match real roles.
Bluesky, live
The public firehose, about 40 posts a second, in a rolling 24-hour window. No answer key: groups are leads.
Bluesky, Iowa
Past posts of accounts around the Iowa election, Oct 27 – Nov 10 2026.
