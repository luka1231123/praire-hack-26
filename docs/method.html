How Behind the Accounts works

It finds groups of accounts that act together, such as troll farms, state operators and spam networks. It uses no text model, because AI can reword every post. It looks at what operators can't easily change: who they reply to, what they link, and when they work.

Why it matters

25,000TikTok accounts in a pro-Russian push before Romania annulled its 2024 election
7,700Facebook accounts in China's Spamouflage network, Meta's largest takedown
3.6Marticles from Russia's Pravda network in 2024, built to seed AI chatbots

Copy-paste used to give these networks away. Now AI rewrites every post, and AI-text detectors fail once text is paraphrased. Read the full purpose.

How it works

Every pair of accounts is scored on five traces:

Same reply targets
They reply to the same messages.
Same timing
They hit the same post, link or tag within 60 seconds.
Same links and tags
They share URLs, hashtags, mentions or reposts. A trace counts 1/(sharers − 1), so a link two accounts share outweighs one shared by fifty.
Same template text
Their posts match once case, links, digits, punctuation and emoji are removed.
Same schedule
Their weekly activity, hour by hour, lines up. This multiplies the other traces.
  1. Every post becomes one row: account, time, text, reply target, links and tags.
  2. Each pair of accounts is scored on the traces above.
  3. Leiden clustering finds tight groups of three or more accounts.
  4. Groups are ranked by shared content per member, with the evidence attached.

Reading a group

Score
Shared content per member. Higher means more rare traces in common.
Schedule match
How alike members' weekly activity is, from 0 to 1.
Kind
The group's topic (political, scam, porn or other), from a small multilingual model that compares sampled posts with example sentences. It describes the topic, not who runs the group.
Bot signs
Bluesky only, from public account facts: same-name, new-accounts, template-bio, blank-profiles, gone-accounts, high-volume (young, many posts a day, few followers), own-domain (links go to a site almost nobody else links) and no-sleep (no quiet hours). Three or more, without disclosure, marks a group hidden and ranks it first.
Real-community signs
likely-community (old accounts with real, varied bios) and disclosed (bios that say bot, feed or official). These rank a group lower.

Limits

It flags behavior, not opinions. It never scores what a post argues. A group can be a real community, a news feed or a fan account network. Open each post at its source and decide yourself.

The time zone shown on a group page comes from its quietest hours. It's a hint, not proof. The live Bluesky view covers only the last 24 hours, so small groups may be chance.

Datasets

Telegram
17.3M comments from 13 Russian channels, with 15,856 accounts labeled as propaganda by researchers (Kireev et al., USENIX Security 2025). Used to measure accuracy.
X: IRA trolls
2.9M tweets from 2,843 accounts of Russia's Internet Research Agency, each tagged with a role (FiveThirtyEight). Used to check that found groups match real roles.
Bluesky, live
The public firehose, about 40 posts a second, in a rolling 24-hour window. No answer key: groups are leads.
Bluesky, Iowa
Past posts of accounts around the Iowa election, Oct 27 – Nov 10 2026.
